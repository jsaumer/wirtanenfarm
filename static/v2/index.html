Eli Wirtanen · 1872–1957

Who was Eli?

“The forest gives what the forest has.”

Eli was born Elias Vertanen in Karstula, Finland, in 1872. As the third son, he had no hope of owning the family farm. As a teenager he emigrated to Thunder Bay, Ontario, to live with his brother David. When David died, Eli came to the United States to apply for citizenship, and in 1904 he homesteaded 40 acres along the Old Vermilion Trail in Markham, Minnesota.

He worked in logging camps, helped local farmers at harvest, and built and maintained roads with horse power. He never married and never owned a car. Nearly everything he needed, from food to the logs for his buildings, came from his own land. Eli died in 1957 and is buried in the Markham Cemetery in Colvin Township.

Our mission

To restore and preserve the pioneer farmstead;

to operate and maintain this farmstead as an educational tool to promote and carry on Finnish immigrant culture and history;

to provide hands-on educational experience in an outdoor setting as an open-air museum;

and to make use of existing historical resources.

Getting there

The entrance is a grass lane off Markham Rd, marked by the carved Eli Wirtanen Pioneer Farm sign at the road’s edge. Park in the gravel lot beside the lane and walk in.
